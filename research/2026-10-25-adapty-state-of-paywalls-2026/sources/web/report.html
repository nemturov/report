Baseline

First, the benchmarks. We look at paywall conversion across regions, how top-performing apps compare, and where users drop off between seeing a paywall and becoming a subscriber.

                What this means for your app

                1

                Don't copy your US paywall across markets

                Category performance changes by region. Test your paywall in each market you sell in.

                2

                Benchmark against the best apps in your region

                The top 10% in every region already outperform the typical US app. Local leaders are a better benchmark.

                Pricing & packaging

                Next, pricing and packaging. We look at what apps charge, which plans they offer, how they use trials and discounts, and how those choices relate to conversion and revenue.

                The typical app charges $6.99/week, $9.99/month, or $39.99/year

                • Annual prices drop outside the US; weekly prices don’t. Annual plans fall from $39.99 in the US to $30.11 in LATAM, while weekly prices stay close to $6.99.
                • Europe is the most expensive region. Median prices reach $8.06/week, $11.55/month, and $40.88/year.
                • Health & Fitness is the cheapest category. The typical plan costs $5.33/week and $29.99/year.

                  Weekly plans bring 62% of subscription revenue

                  • Weekly plans dominate subscription revenue. They account for 62% of revenue overall, compared with 21% for annual and 12% for monthly plans.
                  • The mix changes significantly by region. Weekly plans account for 76% of revenue in LATAM and 70% in MEA, but only 50% in Europe, where annual and monthly plans play a bigger role.
                  • Category matters even more than region. Health & Fitness gets 62% of its revenue from annual plans, while Entertainment gets 81% from weekly plans.

                    • More plans don’t always mean better conversion. In A/B tests, more products win only 53% of the time.

                              Paywall design

                              Next, the paywall itself. Using paywall data from , we look at how top subscription apps structure their paywalls, which elements they use, and what design choices are linked to higher conversion.

                              The typical paywall has two plans, annual pre-selected, and a free trial

                              • Two plans is the standard. 62% of paywalls show two plans, and 73% present their plans as cards.
                              • Annual is the default choice. 67% of paywalls pre-select annual, compared with 16% monthly and 5% weekly.
                              • Most paywalls sell the trial, not the product. 66% offer a free trial, and most make it the CTA. Meanwhile, more than half use no image and only 27% show social proof.

                                            What this means for your app

                                            1

                                            Know when you’re following the default

                                            If your paywall has two plan cards, annual pre-selected, and a free-trial CTA, you’re using the market’s most common setup. That’s a benchmark, not a best practice.

                                            2

                                            Test what most paywalls still ignore

                                            Social proof, awards, trial timelines, weekly price framing, and personalized headlines are still rare. They’re obvious places to look for your next test.

                                            3

                                            Carry onboarding context into the paywall

                                            Almost no paywalls use what the user already told the app. Bring their goal, answers, or choices into the headline and offer instead of showing everyone the same paywall.

                                            4

                                            Decide whether you’re selling the trial or the product

                                            With a trial, paywalls lean on “free” and reassurance. Without one, they work harder to show value. Make that choice deliberately — then test it.

                                            Placement & timing

                                            Next, the timing. We look at when users are most likely to buy, how onboarding paywalls compare with later placements, and what happens after users close the paywall.

                                            The first session wins — but 38% of revenue starts later

                                            • Onboarding still brings most revenue. 53% comes from subscriptions started during the first session, compared with 38% from later paywalls.
                                            • For some categories, later matters just as much. In Lifestyle and Utilities, 48% of revenue starts after the first session — slightly more than during onboarding.
                                            • Monthly subscriptions start later more often. 47% of monthly revenue comes from subscriptions started after the first session, compared with 38% for weekly and 32% for annual.

                                            Where subscription revenue starts

                                              93% of first purchases happen on Day 0

                                              • Day 0 dominates. 93% of first purchases happen within 24 hours of install, generating 89% of first-90-day revenue.
                                              • Monthly buyers take longer to convert. 81% buy on Day 0, compared with 88% of annual and 92% of weekly buyers.
                                              • Later buyers look more valuable — but timing isn’t the reason. They generate 23% more revenue over 90 days, but the gap almost disappears when comparing the same plan. Later buyers simply choose longer plans and direct purchase more often.

                                                      What this means for your app

                                                      1

                                                      Make the first session your strongest monetization moment

                                                      93% of first purchases happen on Day 0, and onboarding paywalls convert almost 4× better than later ones. Put your strongest paywall experience where users are most ready to buy.

                                                      2

                                                      Don’t treat a paywall close as the end

                                                      40% of buyers need more than one paywall view before they purchase. Keep creating relevant reasons to show the paywall again instead of relying on a single shot.

                                                      3

                                                      Build a monetization journey beyond onboarding

                                                      7 in 10 paywall views happen after the first session, and subscriptions started later still generate 38% of revenue. Plan where the next paywall appears — not just the first one.

                                                      4

                                                      Test a second chance after the close

                                                      Only 4% of apps show another offer immediately after a paywall is closed, yet these second-chance paywalls convert at 0.88× the rate of the main one. Test whether a different offer can recover users who weren’t ready the first time.

                                                      Testing & personalization

                                                      Next, the experiments. We look at how often apps test, which paywall changes actually win, and what happens when the experience is personalized to the user.

                                                      A quarter of large apps run 21+ paywall tests a year

                                                      • Large apps test every few weeks. Among apps with 500K+ monthly paywall views, the top quarter run 21+ tests a year. The top 10% run 55+ — more than one a week.
                                                      • You don’t need massive traffic to test regularly. Among apps with 50K–500K monthly paywall views, the top quarter still run 9+ tests a year, and the top 10% run 24+.

                                                        • Visual changes are the weakest bet. Only 35% of visual or copy tests increase LTV, and 31% improve conversion.

                                                                What this means for your app

                                                                1

                                                                Test the offer before the cosmetics

                                                                Localization, trials, and plan changes win more often than visual or copy tweaks. Start with the mechanics of the offer before polishing how it looks.

                                                                2

                                                                Build a testing habit, not a one-off test

                                                                The most active large apps run 21+ tests a year, and even mid-size apps in the top quarter run 9+. Keep a pipeline of experiments instead of waiting for the perfect idea.

                                                                3

                                                                Don’t stop at one paywall

                                                                8 in 10 apps use multiple paywalls, and the number grows with scale. Build different experiences for different moments and audiences instead of trying to make one paywall work for everyone.

                                                                4

                                                                Personalize around the user, not just their device

                                                                Most personalization still stops at country, language, or platform. User goals, onboarding answers, subscription status, and acquisition source give you much richer signals to test.
